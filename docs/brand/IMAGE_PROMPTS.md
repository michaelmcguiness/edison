# Edison Source Assemblage prompts

These are the production prompts used for the first Edison story-art set. Keep
the shared direction stable and replace only the subject brief when expanding
the library.

## Shared direction

> Premium editorial collage for a personalized knowledge publication. Tactile
> hand-cut archival collage: vintage encyclopedia photography mixed with
> painted paper shapes, convincing paper edges, subtle halftone and print
> grain, sophisticated and slightly strange rather than whimsical. Begin in
> warm monochrome: Book Paper #F7F1E5, Midnight Ink #1C2A38, and natural
> photographic greys. Introduce no more than one subject-specific jacket color.
> Oxide #B33B2E may appear once as editorial punctuation, but it is not
> required. The permanent Edison palette must not flatten every subject into
> the same house treatment.
> Portrait orientation approximately 4:5, bold readable composition that
> survives a narrow crop. No words, letters, logos, lightbulbs, books, brains,
> UI, frames, watermark, or gradients.

## Soil / climate archive

> Subject: the hidden climate archive in soil. Show a single cutaway soil core
> as the hero object, with elegant root systems, sediment strata, one tiny
> precision sensor probe, and one surprising human detail: a gloved fingertip
> gently touching a layer. Add one factual-artifact element: a small cropped
> topographic contour diagram on torn paper, with no legible text or numbers.
> Center the hero slightly off-axis and leave thoughtful negative space. The
> result should feel like an intelligent evidence desk made poetic—organic,
> tactile, and editorial, not generic AI surrealism.

## City memory

> Subject: why cities remember what people forget. One hero subject: an
> archival aerial cutout of a dense street intersection seen from above. One
> factual artifact: a torn-paper transit diagram made only of unlabeled lines
> and nodes. One surprising human detail: a small hand placing a single
> building-shaped paper piece. Layer old masonry texture, map fragments, and
> one deep-red registration circle; use generous negative space. Feel like urban archaeology
> laid out on an evidence desk.

## Specialized models

> Subject: the case for smaller, stranger AI models. One hero subject: a
> compact vintage circuit board or laboratory module isolated as a cut-paper
> object, not a robot. One factual artifact: a small unlabeled matrix/grid
> diagram on torn stock. One surprising human detail: a tiny watchmaker's hand
> adjusting one component with tweezers. Add an unexpected organic specimen
> shape to suggest specialization. Use a restrained warm-grey-and-ink ground
> with one controlled Oxide block. Feel precise, niche, curious, and
> editorial.

## Attention

> Subject: attention is a relationship, not a resource. One hero subject: a
> cropped archival profile of a person calmly looking toward a small natural
> specimen, composed as separate paper cutouts. One factual artifact: an
> unlabeled diagram of intersecting orbital lines on torn stock. One surprising
> human detail: a fingertip gently redirecting one paper line. Use a warm-stock
> field, charcoal paper shape, Oxide oval, and subtle ink halftone.
> Feel intimate, psychological, spacious, and intelligent—not wellness
> advertising.

Generated artifacts are editorial illustrations, not documentary evidence.
Any real maps, charts, documents, photographs, or archival material introduced
later must be licensed or public domain and accurately labeled.
